Campus Agentic Transformation Accelerator

A university is a thousand offices a student has to navigate alone. Advising queues, admissions questions at midnight, early alerts that arrive after the withdrawal deadline. This accelerator packages the patterns, the readiness assessment, and the governance pack an institution needs to run Muse agents across the whole student lifecycle, advising, enrollment, student success, and campus services, with FERPA discipline and human advisors where judgment matters.

A working reference artifact by Greg Lucas, founder of AIccountable and creator of agentlint. Greg also built CourseAgent Studio for governed course design; this edition is the institution wide companion, the campus around the course.

Independent portfolio project. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Meta.

Why higher education, why now, and why Muse

Student success is a coordination problem wearing an academic costume. The data to spot a struggling student exists, split across the LMS, the SIS, advising notes, and financial aid. The staff to act on it are booked three weeks out. Agents close the coordination gap: they answer the routine at 2 am, assemble the advisor briefing before the meeting, and surface the early alert while there is still time to act. They do not replace the advisor, the instructor, or the integrity of the credential.

Meta Muse is the natural stack for this work. Muse agents meet students where they already are, in the messaging surfaces they actually use. The Muse API lets an institution compose governed agents for advising, enrollment, and services. Meta Business Agent patterns translate directly to campus service storefronts, and Muse Code helps institutional IT teams build and maintain the integrations. This site maps four institution wide patterns service by service, scores your readiness across the Identify to Prove arc, and maps agentlint style checks to FERPA framed controls.

Not CourseAgent Studio, on purpose

Greg's CourseAgent Studio turns a syllabus into a governed course agent: course design, learning activities, and assessment inside one class. This accelerator deliberately works the other side of the campus: the institution wide services that surround every course. Advising, admissions, student success, financial aid navigation, alumni and campus services. Same governance spine, different altitude. A mature institution runs both: Studio inside the course, this accelerator across the campus.

Four transformation patterns

Each pattern names the student outcome, the agent design, and the Muse stack services it runs on. Every pattern ships with the same governance spine: FERPA framed data handling with education records staying in institutional systems, role based access for students, advisors, and staff, verified authorship on student work through Aithentik (Greg's proof layer from pAIgeBreaker), and complete interaction logging that the institution can review.

1. Academic Advising Agent

A degree planning companion for students and the advisors behind them: the agent answers degree audit questions in plain language, drafts multi term plans against the catalog and the student's record, flags prerequisite chains and bottleneck courses, and books the human advisor with a briefing attached. Advisors approve plan changes. The agent never overrides a degree requirement.

Outcome: advising capacity stretches to the students who never booked an appointment, plans are checked against the catalog every time, and advisors start meetings briefed instead of blind.

Stack: Muse agents for the student facing companion, the Muse API composing degree audit and catalog services the institution exposes, Muse Code for the integration work inside institutional IT, institutional SIS and degree audit systems as the records of record, Aithentik verified advising summaries logged to the student record with student consent.

2. Enrollment and Admissions Assistant

Prospective students and families get real answers at the moment of intent: program fit, deadlines, transfer credit explanations, aid steps, and campus visit booking, in the channels families already use. The assistant drafts follow ups for admissions counselors and routes exceptions, residency questions, aid appeals, anything with judgment in it, to a human with context attached.

Outcome: inquiry response time drops from days to minutes, melt falls as deadlines stop being surprises, and counselors spend their time on students, not on status questions.

Stack: Meta Business Agent patterns for the admissions storefront across Meta surfaces, Muse agents for the guided conversation, the Muse API for CRM and application status integrations, Muse Code for the data plumbing, institutional CRM as the system of record with consent captured at first contact.

3. Student Success Early Alert Copilot

A copilot for advisors and success coaches, not for students: it watches the signals the institution already collects, LMS engagement, missed assignments, registration gaps, aid holds, and assembles a weekly caseload briefing ranked by need, with the evidence for each flag and a drafted outreach for the coach to edit and send. Humans decide who gets contacted and what gets said.

Outcome: alerts arrive while intervention still works, coaches carry larger caseloads without thinner attention, and every outreach is a human message, not an automated nudge.

Stack: Muse API for the signal synthesis and briefing composition, Muse agents for the coach facing workspace, Muse Code for LMS and SIS integration pipelines, institutional data warehouse as the governed data layer, Aithentik style attestation on the evidence pack behind each flag.

4. Campus Services Agent

One front door for the hundred small questions that eat a campus: housing deadlines, dining, IT help, library services, accessibility accommodations routing, and event information, answered from official sources with citations, in the messaging apps students live in. Anything sensitive, accommodations, conduct, wellbeing, routes immediately to the right human office with the student's consent.

Outcome: front desks and help desks shed the repetitive queue, answers stay consistent across offices, and sensitive needs reach humans faster, not slower.

Stack: Meta Business Agent for the campus services storefront, Muse agents for conversation and routing, the Muse API for knowledge grounding over official campus sources, smart glasses pilots for accessibility and wayfinding experiences where the institution chooses to run them, full logging to institutional systems.

          The first 90 days, the way an institutional transformation team would run it

          • Days 1 to 30, Identify and charter: run the census across advising, enrollment, student success, and services with FERPA, accessibility, and integrity leadership in the room. Pick one pattern, advising or enrollment support, tier its student data risk, and sign its charter before any build starts.
          • Days 31 to 60, Design and prove the data: stand up the governed warehouse data and Muse API integrations the pattern needs, with FERPA access controls enforced. Build the proof of concept with accuracy gates against official sources. Train the first cohort of advisors or counselors.
          • Days 61 to 90, Implement and measure: pilot with one college or one entering cohort, human approval on every consequential outcome, full logging from the first interaction. Package the pattern pack, and report Prove metrics, persistence signals, advisor time returned, and accuracy, to the governance body.
